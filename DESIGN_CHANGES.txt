LAST PLATE — CHANGES FROM THE ORIGINAL DESIGN DOCUMENT

The core concept remains the same: move a chef, catch a three-ingredient recipe in order,
tilt the plate to avoid hazards, and survive a 60-second rush with three lives.

Changes made during development:

1. Added named, predefined recipes instead of fully random orders. This gives each order
   more personality and prevents repetitive or confusing combinations.
2. Added target-aware spawning. The next required ingredient is guaranteed to appear
   regularly, so success depends on movement and timing instead of unlucky randomness.
3. Plate tilting now uses a rechargeable energy meter. This makes tilting a limited tactical
   resource rather than an unlimited way to ignore every dangerous item.
4. Added score, catch combos, score multipliers, and rare golden ingredients. Correct play
   is now rewarded beyond the original one-point-per-order system.
5. Added distinct named phases, transition announcements, faster difficulty scaling, wobbling
   ingredients, and more burnt food during Kitchen Chaos.
6. Added an illustrated restaurant-kitchen background, cartoon chef and food artwork,
   a styled order ticket, a tilt meter, stronger color hierarchy, and animated feedback text.
7. Added separate sounds for normal catches, golden catches, tilting, completed orders,
   lost lives, phase changes, and the end of the round. Background music remains intentionally absent.
8. Added a results rank (Line Cook, Sous Chef, or Master Chef), final score, orders served,
   and best combo to make repeat attempts more meaningful.
9. Replaced instant wrong-ingredient damage with a visible three-slot plate inventory. Wrong
   ingredients occupy space and must be intentionally discarded by tilting; carrying more food
   also slows the chef. Burnt food remains immediately dangerous.
10. Replaced the single anonymous order with a named customer ticket and patience system. Only
    one order is shown at a time so the player can focus on catching and preparing it; Dinner Rush
    and Kitchen Chaos increase pressure through faster timing and more demanding customer types.
11. Improved first-play clarity with large recipe icons, a pulsing next-ingredient highlight,
    a visible ingredient stack on the chef's plate, tilt-ready guidance, distinct wrong-catch audio,
    and stronger damage and order-completion flashes. Burnt food remains the only source of life loss.
12. Added original customer portrait artwork, three expressive chef poses, and short spoken chef
    reactions for burnt food, golden catches, and completed orders. Voice cooldowns keep these
    reactions readable without overwhelming the arcade sound effects.
13. Replaced the smaller combined food sheet during gameplay with individual high-resolution,
    transparent ingredient sprites. The procedural plate was also increased from 128x32 to
    512x128 pixels with a cleaner rim and distinct tilted coloring.
14. Replaced the customer timer's late green-to-red color change with a larger fixed-color meter
    that physically shrinks from right to left, making remaining recipe time easier to judge.

These changes move the project away from a basic falling-object clone by making recipe order,
resource management, target selection, risk/reward scoring, and escalating kitchen pressure
the center of play.
